From accounts to interpretation

Follow the interpretation back to the account.

Inspect the full invented excerpt, proposed coding and a plausible alternative explanation. Each episode has one perspective; none has been independently verified.

From interpretation to decision

More projects—or different conditions for action?

The diagnosis can guide management experiments. These interventions are hypotheses to test, not impact forecasts.

A / Transfer

Create an authorised testing window

Reserve line time, name an industrial owner and agree test limits in advance.

Episode contrast: E05 stalls over cost and authorisation; E06 proceeds with explicit time and authority.

Test: follow the next three test requests, including refusals. Record time to decision, time to test and reasons for blockage.

Alternative: technical maturity may explain the difference.

B / Challenge

Give technical objections an explicit consequence

Before committing to delivery, seek an independent objection and record a decision on each risk raised.

Episode contrast: E09 does not incorporate an objection; E10 revises the plan after an adverse test.

Test: observe the next three decision reviews. Record who challenges, the response and actual plan changes.

Alternative: the objection may have lacked adequate support.

C / Continuity

Reserve maintenance between projects

Agree responsibilities, access and minimum availability to sustain a learning cycle after delivery.

Episode contrast: E11 loses activity after funding; E12 maintains tests through bounded commitments.

Test: follow resource access, time used and joint activity for 90 days. Check whether it serves a continuing need.

Alternative: the activity ends because its purpose is fulfilled.

Test a concrete change, observe new episodes and revise the interpretation.

Before/after comparison supports learning but does not isolate causality. Demand, technology, funding and personnel may also explain the result.

How to collect and analyse

The episode is the unit. The interview is a perspective.

CollectDemand, sequence, actors, resources, alternatives and results.
CompareBring together accounts of the same episode; seek disagreements and counterexamples.
InterpretIdentify coordination conditions and review the reading with participants.
TestChange one condition and follow new real situations.

Interview guide / 45 minutes

  1. Which function matters most to the consortium, and why?
  2. Describe a recent occasion when that function was needed. When and where?
  3. What triggered action? What happened first, then next?
  4. Who participated or was left out? Which resources and authorisations were necessary?
  5. Which relationships formed or were reactivated? Which pathways were tried and abandoned?
  6. How were decisions made? What happened to disagreement?
  7. What resulted, for whom and against which criterion? What documentary trace exists?
  8. What continued afterwards? Describe a contrasting occasion.

A first real pilot

One delimited collaboration; 8–12 interviews across different functions and positions; a target of 10–20 unique episodes, without assuming saturation.

Start with three functions relevant to the client. Triangulate selected episodes with another participant and authorised documentation.

Deliver episode records, a comparative synthesis, an interpretation workshop and a plan for a 90-day experiment.

Utility criterion: the client can identify a concrete decision made more informed by the analysis and inspect its supporting accounts.

Coding rules and the role of AI

An LLM can propose fields and locate passages, but each code needs human review and an excerpt reference. Separate explicit, inferred, disputed and unknown information; never complete gaps. Multiple accounts of one event remain one episode. Keep coding revisions and disagreements. Two analysts review a common selection before broader use.

Separate initial demand, intended function, coordination, enabling/blocking conditions, reported result and persistence. Do not infer function from topology or treat a reported outcome as independently established impact.

Limits of the claims

Interviews provide situated accounts with selective memory and differing positions. The relational field is not directly observed. Observed repertoire is conditioned by sample and context; potential capacity requires additional observation or exercises.

The demonstration was constructed to make contrasts visible. It does not validate the theory, automated extraction accuracy or commercial demand. Every episode has one narrator and no triangulation. Visual layouts and organisational representatives are illustrative, not measured.

Responsible use in a real organisation

Agree consent, anonymisation and excerpt access. Share aggregated interpretations by default; identifiable accounts require specific permission. Participants need a way to dispute coding and interpretations.

Inspiration and boundaries

0:00–0:45 / The problem

A consortium can win funding applications and still struggle to move from laboratory to production. Counting partners and projects does not explain where action stalls.

0:45–1:30 / The proposition

We collect episodes of real work and compare how the same actors organise themselves under different demands. We call this observed variety their relational repertoire.

1:30–3:00 / The demonstration

Show “Mobilise”, “Transfer” and “Challenge assumptions”. Open E05 and E06: one prototype stalls, another reaches testing. Ask what changed in the conditions for action—and what other explanations need checking.

3:00–4:15 / The decision

Test an industrial testing window with an owner, reserved time and authority. New episodes allow us to assess whether the change helped.

4:15–5:00 / The invitation

Commission a bounded paid pilot: three functions, interviews and an interpretation workshop. The result should support a decision and a verifiable management experiment.

Make visible the conditions under which collaboration can produce action.

No result here comes from a real client. Price and duration depend on access, interview scope and verification depth.
