A fictional care network

A Decade of Care / Anton’s network

Anton is a recently widowed older adult living with Alzheimer’s disease. Over ten years his support needs change, his family ages, finances shift and paid workers enter and leave.

The twelve accounts sample a changing network; they are not a complete life history. Rosa works in years 1–3 and returns for a paid handover in year 4. Ines is a focal worker in selected later episodes, not a claim of uninterrupted employment. Other temporary workers are described without adding every person to the visual. A faded actor may be unavailable, no longer in the role, or simply unmentioned: read the episode.

Continuity is a collective accomplishment. It depends on decent working conditions, resources, authority and the preferences of the person receiving care.

All names, events, dates and outcomes are invented. This is not a reconstruction of a particular family’s medical history.

Episode interview guide

  1. What needed to be assured on this occasion?
  2. What had changed in the person’s, family’s or workers’ circumstances?
  3. What happened first, then next?
  4. Who was available, who participated, and who was missing?
  5. What knowledge, paid time, money or authority was needed?
  6. How were the person’s preferences included? What remained uncertain?
  7. What was actually provided, and what remained unmet?
  8. Who might tell the story differently? What contrasting event can we reconstruct?

A bounded first pilot

Agree scope with a care partnership, workers and participating families. Select three functions and reconstruct recent episodes across perspectives; do not assume one relative represents everyone.

Seek the person’s supported participation wherever appropriate. Use authorised records and other accounts to check selected events. Keep access to sensitive information proportionate to the task.

Deliverables: episode records, the same repertoire explorer, continuity choices, explicit unresolved needs and a bounded review plan.

What the visual can and cannot establish

All case data are synthetic. Links illustrate coordination narrated in an invented account; positions are design choices. Unmentioned actors are faded, without proving absence. These are selected events from a changing cast, not one fixed network observed continuously.

The method separates required functions, reported configurations and outcomes. It does not establish diagnosis, clinical effectiveness, trust scores, causal intervention effects or adequacy of care. Synthetic examples explain a proposition; they do not validate it.

Responsible interpretation

In real work, agree consent, supported participation, access and pseudonymisation. Retain alternative explanations and worker perspectives. Do not turn the explorer into a staff ranking or assign family responsibilities without agreement. AI may propose codes and retrieve passages, but must not fill gaps or invent relationships.

Five-minute client conversation

You have care services.
Can the network sustain care?

0:00–0:45 / The problem

Ten years change the person, the family, the workers and the resources. A list of services does not explain how care continues through those changes.

0:45–1:30 / The proposition

Reconstruct concrete episodes and compare how the care network notices, provides care, coordinates, transfers knowledge, responds and adapts.

1:30–3:00 / The product

Open “Respond & substitute”. Compare E09 and E10: contacts converge on an unavailable relative; a later event activates a confirmed alternative. Inspect the accounts and the limits of that contrast.

3:00–4:15 / The decision

Open “Sustain & adapt”. Relationships persist while money and availability change. Identify what needs renegotiation, what requires resources and what remains unmet.

4:15–5:00 / The invitation

Commission a bounded pilot around three functions. Collect multiple perspectives, check selected episodes and test one feasible coordination change.
